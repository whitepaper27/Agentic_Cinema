# StudioClear submission recording guide

Target: 2 minutes 50 seconds, with 10 seconds of margin.
Prepared from the repository's current UI and product documentation; this guide
does not establish that a live run succeeded. Record and inspect the actual run.

## Editorial decision

`demo/last-light/scene.txt` is effective as a factual-review demonstration: the
1935 setting, digital-camera screen, deliberately questionable cable caption,
and ambiguous mark give the reviewer distinct tasks. Its rescue ending works.
But three comic pages and seeded mistakes spend precious time explaining a
different workflow from the current scene-to-production product.

Use `demo/submission-scene.txt` for the main submission. This adaptation of the
existing gold-scene demo has a visual hook, two performers, one environment,
two transformations, and a human consequence. The final almost-touch makes
the creative intent easy to remember. No generated film is needed to explain it.

The video story: **A filmmaker has a scene worth protecting. A production
constraint changes. StudioClear helps them choose a way forward and share it.**

## Before recording

1. Open the hosted app in a clean browser window. Hide bookmarks and unrelated
   tabs; disable notifications. Record at 1920 x 1080. Increase browser zoom
   until the cost lines and evidence are readable in the recording.
2. Use your own voice and a quiet room. A brief scene-text opening is enough;
   additional artwork and music are optional. Do not spend the deadline making
   a separate film trailer.
3. Paste the scene from `submission-scene.txt`. Select **Plan this shoot** and
   the live mode. **Try an example** is simulated and cannot prove live research.
4. Paste this instruction:

   > Plan filming for this single fantasy scene. Preserve both gold transformations,
   > the almost-touch ending, and all three spoken lines exactly. Two adult
   > performers remain on dry ground; there is no boat or water-entry shot.
   > Compare a nearby practical location, an alternative travel location, and
   > local filming with a recreated coastal background. Every approach must
   > account for the gold effects. Use my production brief for cost assumptions
   > and distinguish them from retrieved evidence and missing quotes.

5. Check **Confirm what we read**, the full scene, and dialogue locks. Click
   **Confirm and plan the shoot**. In **Shoot options**, enter your brief.
6. For a reproducible fictional producer scenario, use the following assumptions.
   These are editorial demo choices, not facts about your production:

   | Field | Demo assumption |
   | --- | --- |
   | Base city / country | Los Angeles / United States |
   | Currency | USD |
   | Alternative travel region | Portugal |
   | Shoot / prep / travel days | 2 / 1 / 2 |
   | Traveling crew / local crew | 4 / 4 initially |
   | Accommodation nights | 3 |
   | VFX shots | 2: clasp transformation and apple transformation |
   | Budget ceiling | Leave blank unless you have a meaningful ceiling |

   These are crew counts, not cast counts. Inspect whether the model covers
   performer fees, studio/background work, props, and all effects. Two
   transformation shots do not describe the additional background compositing
   the local/VFX approach might require. Explain exclusions if unmodeled.

7. Open the editable rate assumptions. Review every prefilled value and visibly
   identify any retained values as **illustrative producer estimates**. Use your
   actual estimates if available. Do not call a search result a supplier quote.
8. Click **Suggest alternative locations** if useful; inspect the leads. Keep
   the comparison within the intended two jurisdictions. Click **Compare three
   options** and inspect all three cards, a substantive source, and cost coverage.
9. Rehearse changing **Traveling crew** from **4 to 8**. This models four additional
   traveling crew members for the travel approach; keep local crew unchanged.
   Click **Recalculate** and inspect exactly which lines changed. If the actual
   staffing interpretation differs, explain that interpretation instead.
10. Select an approach for a reason supported by the result. Test **Print / Save
    PDF** and **Download production brief (JSON)**. Open the exported PDF and
    check that the selected approach, amounts, and source references match.
11. Capture the full successful live session, including processing waits. Keep
    that original. In the edit, label removed waiting time as "Processing wait
    shortened"; do not imply that edited runtime is processing latency.

If the result omits required effects, has irrelevant evidence, or loses the
selection on export, fix that path before recording claims about it. A visible
remaining quote gap is useful evidence of the product's limits.

## Exact recording sequence and voiceover

Read naturally and pause for the viewer to see each result. The script leaves
space for clicks and source inspection. Bracketed text must be replaced after
rehearsal with observations from the recorded run.

### 0:00-0:15 — Make the scene matter

Show the scene title, the apple transformation, and "Don't touch me." as three
short text reveals. Move into the actual app by 0:12.

Say:

> She finds a stone that turns everything she touches to gold. Then the person
> she loves reaches for her hand. As a filmmaker, how do I keep this moment
> while choosing a practical way to shoot it?

### 0:15-0:35 — Give the producer control

Show the pasted scene, **Plan this shoot**, then **Confirm what we read**.
Highlight the dialogue lock for "Don't touch me." Click **Confirm and plan
the shoot**. Trim typing, keeping the actual confirmation visible.

Say:

> This is StudioClear. I bring in the scene, and Gemini reads it for production
> planning. I confirm the scene and lock the dialogue. The magic and the ending
> are creative requirements we need to preserve.

### 0:35-0:55 — Establish the decision

Show the base, alternative region, crew count, days, and rate assumptions.
Click **Compare three options**. Keep the estimate label readable.

Say:

> Our demo production is based in Los Angeles. We're comparing a nearby
> location, travel to Portugal, and local filming with VFX. These rates are
> illustrative producer estimates. StudioClear uses the brief to make the
> assumptions behind each option visible.

### 0:55-1:20 — Prove research has a purpose

Show the three actual option cards. Open one relevant retrieved source in the
app, or follow its link if there is no source detail panel. Zoom to the passage
that supports the lead you describe. Return to its corresponding cost/assumption.

Say:

> Parallel searches for production leads behind these options. Here is
> [the source and its specific relevant finding]. That gives us [the supported
> planning implication]. The cost table separately shows our rate assumptions.
> A lead still needs confirmation before we can rely on it for the shoot.

Keep the inserted source explanation under 20 words total if possible. Choose
a source that actually affects the decision. A generic country page is weak proof.

### 1:20-1:50 — The memorable product moment

Hold the original travel amount long enough to read. Change **Traveling crew**
from **4 to 8** and click **Recalculate**. Show the updated travel amount and
one affected cost line. If using before/after overlays, transcribe actual values.

Say:

> Now the producer needs four more people traveling with the shoot. I change
> one assumption and recalculate. [Name the cost lines that actually changed.]
> The travel estimate moves from [observed range] to [observed range]. We can
> see the production consequence of that decision immediately in the comparison.

Do not promise a recommendation change. If ranges overlap, say "The ranges
still overlap, so there isn't a clear cost winner." If prices are missing,
call the amount a known-cost subtotal. Use the appropriate branch only.

### 1:50-2:15 — Make a human decision

Show one actual gap or tradeoff. Click **Select for planning** on the option
you can justify. Enter a concise reason if prompted.

Say:

> For this version of the brief, I select [actual option] because [supported
> reason]. We still need to confirm [actual unresolved item]. The comparison
> helps me explain that choice while keeping the scene's creative requirements
> in view.

Possible rationale, only when supported: "The nearby option avoids the added
travel expense and retains a real coastline." A selection can be based on
creative preference even when cost ranges overlap; say so clearly.

### 2:15-2:35 — Finish the workflow

Click **Print / Save PDF**, save it, and open the actual exported brief.
Show the selection, assumptions, and evidence. Briefly show the JSON download
button if it fits; do not scroll through raw JSON in the main video.

Say:

> I export the production brief with the selected approach, alternatives,
> assumptions, and research. This is the document I can hand to a collaborator
> so they can see the decision and what we still need to confirm.

### 2:35-2:50 — Explain the technology and land the promise

Over actual app footage, display a small text overlay:
"Gemini: scene understanding | Parallel: research | Python: cost calculations
| Google Cloud Run: hosting." Use an actual relevant execution record if
available; do not create an imitation log or claim a trace proves unshown calls.
End with StudioClear, the hosted URL, and the line below.

Say:

> Gemini understands the scene. Parallel supplies research. Python calculates
> the costs, and the app runs on Google Cloud. StudioClear: one scene, three
> ways to film it, and a production decision you can explain.

## Editing and final check

- Keep most of the video on the functioning product. Use close crops for text,
  steady cursor movements, and brief pauses after the recalculation and export.
- Add English captions. Keep background audio low enough that every word is clear.
- Keep the same scene and run throughout. Any opening preview should be labeled.
- Remove placeholders and read numerical claims against the recorded screen.
  Do not reuse figures from a different brief or from submission prose.
- `SUBMISSION.md` contains a claimed live run dated September 9, 2026, later
  than this session's September 8 date. Those numbers are not independently
  verified here; use the footage and exports of your own run as evidence.
- If adding a user-impact sentence, use an actual observed result with participant
  count and context. Otherwise finish with the demonstrated planning outcome.
- Publish the video and test its link signed out, then add it to the submission.

## Official submission requirements checked September 8, 2026

The [official rules](https://agentic-cinema.devpost.com/rules) specify a video
of at most three minutes (only the first three are evaluated), publicly visible
on YouTube or Vimeo, in English or with English subtitles, showing the working
project. The deadline is September 9 at 2 PM Pacific, or 4 PM Chicago time.

The four equally weighted criteria are technological implementation, design,
potential impact, and quality of the idea. This script addresses them through
the research/calculation proof, complete workflow, producer decision, and
preservation of creative intent. Parallel Search must actually run in the project.

The rules restrict AI tooling to Google Cloud and the selected partner's
permitted features. This recording plan is AI-assisted; do not treat it as an
eligibility determination. Resolve the applicability to preparation assistance
with the organizer if needed, and retain truthful tooling provenance.

No script guarantees a prize. A clear, functioning demonstration with claims
the viewer can verify gives this project its strongest presentation.
