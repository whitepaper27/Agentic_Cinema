# Reviewer notes — keep out of app input

These are human rehearsal leads, not runtime sources and not prefilled verdicts. Last checked September 7, 2026. The app must retrieve evidence itself, record actual query/time/provider, and assess applicability. No live app performance has been measured for this pack.

## 1. Visual equipment issue

Expected question: Is a compact digital camera with a rear electronic color display appropriate in an ordinary 1935 harbor scene?

Source lead: [Kodak photography history](https://www.kodak.com/en/company/page/photography-history/) dates its digital-camera invention to 1975. This supports investigating the obvious era mismatch, but it is not evidence for a particular replacement camera or an exact invention date for every displayed feature. Do not claim that today's compact design existed in 1975.

Useful output: a period-mismatch finding tied to page 1/panel 2 and an actionable art note. Research a specific replacement independently before asserting its availability. A conservative action-description revision may remove unsupported specificity, but cannot certify a replacement prop. Editing text never removes the electronic screen from the original drawing.

## 2. Caption correction — the primary accept/recheck demo

Input narration deliberately says the first transatlantic telegraph cable was in 1868. Expected researched correction: 1858, preserving the rest of the caption and all spoken dialogue.

Source leads:

- [IET archives: first transatlantic telegraph cable, 1858](https://www.theiet.org/membership/library-and-archives/the-iet-archives/archives-highlights/the-first-transatlantic-telegraph-cable-1858)
- [Science Museum Group: sample of first transatlantic cable, 1857–1858](https://collection.sciencemuseumgroup.org.uk/objects/co33447/sample-of-deep-sea-section-of-first-transatlantic-cable-1857-1858)

Distinguish the first cable from later durable service. Do not embellish the revision with an unsupported claim of uninterrupted operation since 1858. The source supporting acceptance must survive in the export under its originating run/source tuple after recheck.

## 3. Ambiguous mark — demonstrate restraint

The authoring brief invents an indistinct mark, but a vision model viewing only the artwork has no basis to infer a real organization, rights owner, or historical affiliation. Correct behavior is to state identification limits, request context if relevant, or mark it out of scope under the historical-props instruction. An explicit creator declaration can be stored separately as user-supplied provenance. Do not force an UNRESOLVED status if the actual supported workflow justifiably treats it as not researched; do not equate either outcome with legal clearance.

## 4. Regression beyond the rehearsed correction

In a separate non-showcase run, add a new factual sentence to the canonical scene through the editor. Verify the recheck extracts it, records its lineage, and researches it or explicitly records why it was not researched. Repeat on a different scene for held-out evaluation. Success on this prepared three-page story is not general accuracy evidence.
