LAST LIGHT

STORY CONTEXT
Grounded historical rescue drama set in 1938 at an invented Atlantic harbor.
The characters and signal house are fictional. There is no time travel,
alternate history, or intentional futuristic technology.

SCENE 1 — PAGE 1: THE PROMISE
EXT. FICTIONAL HARBOR SIGNAL HOUSE - NIGHT

CAPTION
1936. A harbor on the Atlantic coast.

PANEL 1
Rain sweeps across a stone signal house above a black harbor. Beyond the harbor wall, a stranded boat shows a faint light. Elsie, a young signal clerk in a charcoal wool coat and rust scarf, holds the door open. Arun, a rescue courier in an olive oilskin coat with a tan satchel, waits on the steps. The signal house lamp above them is dark.

PANEL 2
Inside, Elsie photographs a water-damaged logbook. The camera is a compact digital model: its illuminated rear color screen clearly displays the same handwritten page. No manufacturer or logo is visible. The handwritten logbook marks are not readable.

PANEL 3
Elsie hands Arun a folded message. Their fingers meet over the paper as rain blows through the doorway.

ELSIE
Keep the light on. I promised I would return.

SCENE 2 — PAGE 2: THE WIRE
INT. SIGNAL HOUSE - CONTINUOUS

PANEL 1
Close view of a brass telegraph key, cloth-covered wire, and an unlettered paper strip on a scarred wooden desk.

CAPTION
Since the first transatlantic telegraph cable in 1868, strangers had trusted a wire to carry hope.

PANEL 2
Arun leans over the desk while Elsie listens to the receiver. A window behind them frames a distant boat's small light.

ARUN
Then let one more message through.

PANEL 3
Elsie pulls open the heavy door to the upper lamp room. A chipped stone inset carries an indistinct angular mark: two uneven arcs and a short broken stroke. It has no readable letters. Arun studies it but offers no identification.

ELSIE
That mark was here before us.

SCENE 3 — PAGE 3: THE ANSWER
INT. SIGNAL HOUSE LAMP ROOM / EXT. HARBOR - CONTINUOUS

PANEL 1
Elsie and Arun turn a large metal wheel beneath the lamp. Warm light rises across their faces. Rain runs down the windows.

PANEL 2
Arun looks through binoculars at a small boat beyond the harbor wall. A figure on deck raises an arm.

ARUN
I can see them.

PANEL 3
A wide final image: the lamp's broad beam cuts through the rain toward the boat. Elsie and Arun stand together behind the lamp-room glass.

ELSIE
Then they can see us.
